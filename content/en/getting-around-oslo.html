Public transport in Oslo is organised by Ruter, which is responsible for the buses, metro, trams and local boats. The local and regional trains are run by Vy, and Flytoget runs the airport express. The line numbers follow a simple pattern, and once you know it, a departure board is easy to read.

How the lines are numbered

  • Metro (T-bane): lines 1 to 5.
  • Trams (trikk): 12, 13, 15, 17, 18 and 19.
  • Buses: 20 and up, such as 20 between Skøyen and Galgeberg or 37 between Nydalen and Helsfyr.
  • Boats: a B in front, such as B10 and B11 to Nesoddtangen.
  • Trains: a letter or two in front. L is a local train, R a regional train and RE a regional express.
  • Airport: FLY1 and FLY2 for Flytoget, FB1, FB3 and FB5 for the airport buses.

Some bus numbers carry a letter after them. A B after a metro or tram number, as in 2B or 13B, is often a replacement bus for that line during engineering work. Check the destination on the front of the bus when the number looks unfamiliar.

The metro

The metro, T-banen, has five lines. All five run through the same tunnel under the city centre, between Majorstuen and Tøyen. The stations in that tunnel are Majorstuen, Nationaltheatret, Stortinget, Jernbanetorget, Grønland and Tøyen. If you are only going between two of them, take the first train that comes, whatever the number.

Outside the centre the lines split up:

  • Line 1 climbs from Majorstuen to Holmenkollen and Frognerseteren. In the east it runs to Bergkrystallen, though many trains turn at Helsfyr.
  • Line 2 runs from Østerås in Bærum via Røa and the centre to Furuset and Ellingsrudåsen.
  • Line 3 runs from Kolsås in Bærum to Mortensrud.
  • Line 4 runs from Bergkrystallen through the centre and round the Ring via Ullevål stadion, Nydalen and Storo to Vestli.
  • Line 5 runs between Vestli and Sognsvann, and passes through the centre twice on the way, as it also does the full Ring.

Jernbanetorget is next to Oslo S, the main railway station. Nationaltheatret has both a metro station and a railway station. The Norwegian guide T-banen i Oslo goes through each line and where they split.

Trams

Six tram lines run in three pairs that share long stretches of track:

  • 12 and 15 both run from Kjelsås via Storo, Torshov and Grünerløkka to Jernbanetorget, and both end at Majorstuen. 12 goes via Aker brygge, 15 via Nationaltheatret.
  • 17 and 18 both currently run between Grefsen stasjon and Gaustadalléen, through the centre and past Bislett, Ullevål sykehus and Blindern. 17 comes in via Carl Berners plass, 18 via Torshov and Grünerløkka.
  • 13 and 19 share the Ekeberg line from Ljabru down to Bjørvika. 19 then goes via Jernbanetorget and Bogstadveien to Majorstuen. 13 does not stop at Jernbanetorget; it runs via Nationaltheatret and Solli to Thune.

Line 19 is the only tram on Bogstadveien, the shopping street up to Majorstuen. Line 12 is the only one that stops at Aker brygge. More in the Norwegian guide Trikken i Oslo.

Buses

Buses fill in everywhere the metro and trams don't go, and with several hundred lines in the Ruter area they are the hardest part to get an overview of. The live map draws a bus route only when you click the bus, which keeps the map readable. To find which buses serve a stop, search for the stop on the map and open its departure board.

Boats

Ruter's boats are part of the same network. B10 runs between Aker brygge and Nesoddtangen, B11 between Lysaker and Nesoddtangen, and B20 between Aker brygge and Slemmestad. B1 serves the islands in the inner Oslofjord.

Local and regional trains

Vy runs the trains around Oslo. Local trains stop at almost every station near the city, regional trains skip some of them, and regional express trains go further and stop less often. All lines call at Oslo S, and most continue west through the tunnel to Nationaltheatret, Skøyen and Lysaker.

  • L1 runs between Spikkestad and Lillestrøm, stopping at the small stations on both sides of the city.
  • L2 runs between Stabekk and Ski, along the Østfold line south of Oslo S.
  • R12, RE10 and RE11 stop at Oslo Airport, as well as Flytoget. See Airport train or bus.
  • RE30 runs from Oslo S to Gjøvik and stops at Grefsen, Nydalen and Kjelsås within Oslo.

Between Oslo S and Lysaker you can usually take the first westbound train. The Norwegian guide Lokaltog og regiontog rundt Oslo covers every line.

Tickets

Buy tickets for buses, metro, trams and boats in the Ruter app. For Vy trains, use the Vy app. Flytoget and the airport buses sell their own tickets. livetrafikk.no does not sell tickets and has no prices.

Seeing it live

The live map shows the vehicles moving. Metro, tram, bus and boat positions are estimated from departure times, as Ruter does not publish GPS positions; trains report their own. How to read the map is explained on the English map page.
